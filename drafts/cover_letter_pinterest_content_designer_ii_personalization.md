Re: Content Designer II, Personalization

I design content systems for personalization work that has to walk a specific line: relevant enough to matter, restrained enough to still feel like discovery. That's the tension your Personalization team is solving for on Homefeed, and it's the work I've spent years doing at Credit Karma.

At Credit Karma, I led content design for a personalization system called Stories, which used four distinct content types to surface relevant financial opportunities across 100M+ members without the experience feeling invasive. Partway through, the team assumed that showing more personalized offers would cannibalize revenue by feeling too pushy. Testing showed the opposite: personalized, contextual content increased engagement and revenue at the same time. That finding shaped how the team approached personalization from then on, and it's the same instinct I'd bring to Homefeed.

I also built a behavioral-science framework that separated two experience types on Credit Karma Money's dashboard: one for discovery, one for management. Getting that distinction right in the content model, not just the visual design, drove real gains in both feature discovery and adoption. Personalization content design lives in that same space: helping someone understand why they're seeing something, not just that they're seeing it.

I've also been hands-on with AI tools throughout my content design process, from prototyping to research synthesis. I treat that as part of the craft.

I'd like to bring that experience to Pinterest's Personalization team.
